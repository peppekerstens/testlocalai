**Tradeoff — Option A (obfuscate phone numbers and note/description text):**
Gained: consistency with the stated privacy intent, and closure of a real gap — phone numbers are direct PII and note/description bodies frequently contain embedded PII (names, addresses, callback numbers) that a field-level policy currently misses entirely since it only screens discrete fields, not prose. Lost: functional utility. A phone number exists specifically so a human or downstream tool can *contact* the person — redacting it defeats that purpose outright, unlike redacting a name or address which are more often just contextual identifiers. Note/description text is where the actual technical substance of a support ticket lives (error messages, steps to reproduce, environment details); reliably scrubbing PII out of free-form prose without also mangling that substance is a much harder problem than obfuscating a structured field — it requires something closer to NER/pattern-matching over unstructured text, with real risk of either over-redacting (destroying the ticket's diagnostic value) or under-redacting (missing PII in phrasing the pattern didn't anticipate), which can produce a false sense of protection that's arguably worse than no redaction at all.

**Tradeoff — Option B (leave as documented passthrough):**
Gained: the tool stays functionally useful for its actual job — support/troubleshooting workflows need the phone number to reach someone and need the note text intact to understand the problem. It also avoids shipping a fragile, easily-bypassed free-text redaction that implies a privacy guarantee the implementation can't reliably deliver. Lost: a real, acknowledged privacy exposure remains — phone numbers pass through raw, and notes can carry PII (names, addresses, other numbers) that the current name/address/email field-level policy was never designed to catch, since it only looks at discrete structured fields.

**Recommendation: Option B.**

Justification: the two items being proposed for obfuscation are not symmetric with the existing name/address/email rules the way Option A frames them. Name/address/email are typically incidental identity fields — redacting them costs little functional value. Phone numbers and note/description text are load-bearing for the tool's actual purpose (contacting people, understanding the technical issue), and note text in particular can't be safely obfuscated with the same "field-level" approach used for names/addresses/emails — it would require a fundamentally different, higher-risk redaction mechanism (pattern/NER matching over prose) that risks both destroying diagnostic value and creating a false sense of security if it misses PII the pattern didn't anticipate. Given that the current behavior is already documented, the better move is to formally confirm this as an intentional, narrower scope (Option B) rather than attempt a blanket redaction (Option A) that trades away the tool's core utility for a privacy guarantee it likely can't reliably keep for unstructured text. This does not eliminate the underlying privacy cost — phone numbers and PII embedded in notes genuinely do leak under B — but that cost is the more honest and manageable one compared to shipping fragile free-text redaction under a false consistency claim.
