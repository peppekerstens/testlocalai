Phone numbers: Obfuscating contact phone numbers would reduce the exposure of a direct personal contact field, but it would also remove the ability to actually contact the person from the ticket. Because the facts identify these as contact phone numbers, and the current documented behavior is that they are never obfuscated, the functional loss from obfuscation is direct: the number would no longer be usable for contact. The privacy gain is real, but it comes at the cost of breaking the apparent purpose of the field.

Note/description text: Ticket note and description text is different from a phone number because it is free-form prose that may contain the actual technical detail needed to answer a support question, such as symptoms, error messages, steps already taken, or environment details. Obfuscating that text with the same treatment as names, addresses, and emails would risk destroying or corrupting the very information that makes the ticket actionable. The privacy benefit is less targeted, because the text may contain many kinds of information, and the loss is a direct reduction in support usefulness.

Recommendation: Option B. Explicitly, I recommend Option B for phone numbers and Option B for note/description text. The recommendation is justified by the facts as given: both are currently documented as passthrough, the project’s sensitivity list does not explicitly include them, and obfuscating them would impair either the ability to contact someone or the ability to use the technical detail in the ticket.
